Here are the specific updates you should make to your terms and privacy pages:

1. The "Non-Sharing" Statement (Privacy Policy)
This is the most common reason for rejection. Your privacy policy must include a clause that explicitly mentions SMS/mobile data.

Required Phrasing: "Mobile information will not be shared with third parties/affiliates for marketing or promotional purposes. All the above categories exclude text messaging originator opt-in data and consent; this information will not be shared with any third parties."

Why: Even if you don’t share data, carriers require this specific disclaimer. Rejections happen if this is missing or even slightly vague.

2. Explicit Opt-In/Opt-Out Flow (Terms of Service)
Your terms should clearly outline how someone joins and leaves the "Zarchbot" list.

Opt-In: State exactly how they sign up (e.g., "Users opt-in by providing their number on a physical sign-up sheet" or "via a checkbox on our website").

The "Four Essentials": You must list these four things clearly on the page:

Brand Name: Clearly identify "Zarchbot" as the sender.

Message Frequency: Use the phrase "Message frequency varies."

Rates Disclosure: Use the phrase "Message and data rates may apply."

Help/Stop: Include "Reply STOP to cancel" and "Reply HELP for help."

3. Special Consideration for Youth Groups
Since your goal involves a youth group (and likely minors like your 14-year-old), carriers are sensitive to age-gating and consent.

Consent for Minors: Your terms should specify that for users under 18, consent must be provided by a parent or guardian.

Call to Action: When you describe your "Message Flow" in the Twilio console, be very specific.

Bad Description: "I'm a hobbyist sending alerts."

Good Description: "Zarchbot sends schedule updates and event notifications to members of a local youth group. Users provide their phone numbers via a non-mandatory physical sign-up form during weekly meetings where they explicitly check a box to receive SMS alerts."

4. Brand Registration (LLC vs. Sole Prop)
Since you established Atrisa Services LLC, you should register as a Standard or Low-Volume Standard brand using your EIN. If you register as a "Sole Proprietor" (intended for those without a Tax ID), you run a high risk of rejection because the carriers' "Trust Registry" may see your business registration in Utah and flag the mismatch.
